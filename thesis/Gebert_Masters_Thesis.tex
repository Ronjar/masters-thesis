\renewcommand{\myAbstract}{
This thesis investigates the causal environmental effects of post-colonial resettlement using settlement schemes in Kenya.
For this purpose, an extensive dataset of historic settlement scheme boundaries is combined with high-resolution satellite imagery of vegetation cover, especially forest cover.
By applying a spatial regression discontinuity design (Spatial RDD), the study shows that allocation of land for settlement schemes has a significant and permanent negative impact on vegetation cover, measured by NDVI and NDMI.
Furthermore, the study finds evidence of a spillover effect, contributing to a higher level of deforestation in adjacent areas.
In contrast to expectations, no evidence of a significant effect of land tenure security, measured by time between allocation and registration, on vegetation cover is found.
These results highlight the importance of considering environmental consequences when implementing resettlement programs, as the ecological costs exceed the plot boundaries, affecting surrounding areas, and undermining long-term sustainability.
}
